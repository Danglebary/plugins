# A stated honesty contract for what agents claim to have checked

From a deep review of the `work-plugins` fork, whose spec 004 shipped an evidence contract whose central rule is one sentence: **a claim is recorded as validated only against evidence actually read**, and any surface that was unavailable, denied, or simply not consulted makes the verdict **explicitly partial**, naming the surface and what it would have confirmed. Its own framing is the useful test — *"three surfaces were configured and two agreed" is a valid partial verdict; "validated" with a surface silently unread is the drift this rule exists to prevent.* The corollary is subtler and catches more: **an empty read is evidence only with its resolution context confirmed** — a query that returned nothing may mean the thing doesn't exist, or may mean it was aimed at the wrong place, and those are recorded differently.

This repo already applies the idea in exactly one place and nowhere else. `IMPLEMENTATION-LOOP.md` pins green as "a **positive** signal, never an assumed one," with a three-source resolution ladder and an explicit degrade when nothing resolves. That is the honesty rule, correctly stated, for one specific claim. Every *other* claim-making surface in the plugin — the deviation fact-checker, the spec-conformance judge, and thirteen reviewer agents — asserts findings against a diff with no stated contract about what counts as having checked, and no vocabulary for a partial verdict at all. A reviewer that skimmed the diff and one that traced the call path through the surrounding module produce output in the same register.

There is direct local evidence that this is a live problem rather than an imported one. `docs/reviews/2026-07-08-whole-plugin-deep-dive.md` records 219 findings from a 118-agent pass; of the 97 rated critical or major, 87 were adversarially verified and **31 were downgraded and 1 refuted** — roughly a third of the high-severity claims did not survive contact with verification. The fork saw the same shape from the other direction: its fresh-context refuters, run deliberately on a *different model* than the writers, repeatedly caught over-generalizations that the writers were structurally unable to see, including a clause that had quietly disclaimed half of a contract's consumers.

The concrete shape is small. A `_shared` doc stating the honesty rule, the partial-verdict form, the empty-read rule, and a **verification tier** vocabulary — the fork's version distinguishes a claim confirmed against documentation from one confirmed by actually firing the operation, and requires an unfired claim to register a **named completing residual**: which future event will complete it, so the debt has an owner rather than evaporating. Then the claim-making agents cite it: `AGENT-FORMAT.md` requires findings to carry what was actually read, reviewers gain a partial-verdict register, and the close-out pair's output sections distinguish "checked and clean" from "not checked." The fork's discipline note is worth carrying too — precedent lowers the surprise but not the bookkeeping bar, so each reuse of a tier is still its own recorded deviation, and "verified" is always a dated, box-relative snapshot rather than a timeless property.

This is the lightest of the ideas banked from that review and the most independent — it touches no lifecycle mechanics, competes with nothing, and would compose cleanly with either the gate audit or the restructure. Open questions at promotion: whether the tier vocabulary is genuinely needed for a local files-store repo where almost everything *is* directly checkable (the fork's tiers exist largely because it authors against a credential-less box), or whether the honesty rule plus the partial-verdict form is the whole of it; and whether reviewer findings gain a required "what I read" field, which raises the cost of every finding and should be priced against the third-of-findings downgrade rate the review pass measured.
